# PRD Quality Review — Peerless

*Reviewed 2026-09-29 against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Scope: `prd.md` (958 lines), `addendum.md`, cross-checked against the four authoritative documents and `analysis/output/industry-screening.md`.*

## Overall verdict

This is a substantive PRD, not furniture. It has a stated thesis that can come back negative (§10), non-goals that give reasons rather than lists, counter-metrics that genuinely counterbalance, and every illustrative figure in §2.3 traces exactly to `analysis/output/industry-screening.md` — which matters for a document read by a sensor checking arithmetic. What is at risk is buildability, not credibility: **Done-ness is thin.** Twelve of sixty-seven FRs carry no testable consequence, three of them load-bearing funnel stages; FR-24 directly contradicts FR-22; and three FRs assert decisions that §11 still lists as open. A story-creation pass would stall on §4.2 stages 3–4 and on §4.7 while sailing through §4.4 and §4.6.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

The decision architecture is better than most: §11 splits questions into **Answered** (with dates and the FR each became), **Gated on measurement**, and **Pre-deployment gates** — three genuinely different kinds of openness, and the distinction is respected. §8's *"The one licence gap, and it is not small"* is the strongest passage in the document: it names what is unverified, says the register's own pages do not settle it, cites `åndsverkloven §§33–34`, and then rules on stakes — *"Not a v1 blocker … and a gate before any public or commercial deployment, where the answer comes from asking Brønnøysundregistrene rather than from reading their website."* That is a decision with what was given up stated. §6.2's cut order does the same work: *"Never the labelled set, the authorisation suite or the OCR measurement — they are what the project's results rest on."*

Where it slips is the seam between §4 and §11. Three FRs read as settled where §11 says otherwise, and an engineer reading only §4 would build something the PM has not chosen. The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land on a product promise (FR-6) and on scope creep (§6.2) — both real, but neither is the sharpest tension in the document. The tensions below carry no callout at all.

### Findings
- **high** FR-13 states banding as decided while OQ-12 calls it open (§4.2 FR-13 / §11.12) — FR-13 asserts cost of goods share and personnel cost share *"enter selection **only in coarse bands** and are benchmarked within their band"* and even specifies a test for within-band spread. OQ-12 asks *"whether personnel cost share enters selection at all, in bands, or not"* and notes *"the decision points still list it as open, with a stated risk that bands blur the benchmark."* One of these is wrong. *Fix:* either close OQ-12 to match FR-13, or add `[NOTE FOR PM]` to FR-13 marking the banding of personnel cost share as provisional pending SM-1's per-layer result.
- **high** FR-8 and FR-18 pull against each other, and OQ-13 admits it (§4.2 FR-8, FR-18 / §11.13) — FR-8's consequence is absolute: *"A candidate failing any comparability field cannot appear in the peer group by any later stage."* FR-18 says *"The user can widen a criterion when the group is too small"* without naming which criteria are widenable. OQ-13 asks *"which comparability criteria are hard exclusions rather than flags."* As written, FR-18 either has an empty set of loosenable criteria or it breaks FR-8. *Fix:* FR-18 lists the loosenable criteria explicitly (size band, segmentation, industry-code adjacency) and states that the six comparability fields in FR-8 are never among them.
- **medium** `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at safe checkpoints (§4.1 FR-6, §6.2) — two callouts across 958 lines, both on tensions already resolved elsewhere in the text. None marks FR-24's contradiction with FR-22, the FR-13/OQ-12 conflict, or the filing-window interaction below. *Fix:* add callouts at the three tensions named in this review; drop nothing.

## Substance over theater — strong

Four personas, each earning its place. UJ-4 is the only source of FR-67 and FR-33–38; UJ-3 is why FR-44–47 distinguish *"between two workspaces held by the same owner"* from between-user isolation; UJ-2's *"his next wish is 'show me every firm in `69.202` with a weak margin'"* is why §5 excludes screening with a harvesting reason rather than a scoping one. UJ-1's **"Weak ending, on purpose"** — *"nothing will tell her it arrived"* — is a persona used to expose a product hole rather than to flatter one.

The Vision does not swap. *"A managing director knows their gross margin. They do not know whether it is good."* is this product; so is *"a comparison against the wrong set is worse than no comparison, because a managing director will spot it in the first thirty seconds and never return."*

The NFRs in §7 have bounds, not adjectives, in every case but one: integer øre with rounding at display only; pure functions with no framework imports; RLS keyed to workspace membership with the attacker list enumerated; 375px with no horizontal body scroll. §10's *"Anticipated findings that are results, not failures"* pre-commits to reporting that the model was needed less than expected — the opposite of innovation theater.

I verified the §2.3 figures rather than trusting the disclaimer. 62.100 median revenue/employee 1 390 898 kr, margin median −3.2 %, Q3 9.1 %; 69.202 1 159 406 kr, median 9.7 %, Q3 15.3 % — all exact against `industry-screening.md` lines 77–78 and 147–148. The derived kroner (7.0 % × 16.7 m = 1.17 m; 12.8 pts × 10.4 m = 1.34 m) are right, and both use the favourable quartile as target per FR-31 rather than the median. The §10 description-quality proxies (56 % / 38 % / 50 %) match line-for-line. §6.1's *"about 1 000"* and *"about 800"* are 1003 and 797.

No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated, falsifiable and load-bearing: *"that reading what a company actually does — from its accounts and from free text — assembles a better peer group than industry classification alone. The measurement is the result, and it can come back negative."* §10 then builds the apparatus to test it — blind labelling with the conflict disclosed (*"the labeller also designed the method"*), an industry-code baseline on the same set, layer-by-layer ablation.

Prioritisation follows the thesis, not ease. §6.2's cut order removes the portfolio page and industry overviews before touching the labelled set; §6.1 gates coverage on measurement per industry; SM-C4 exists specifically to resist adding a third industry. The counter-metrics are real: SM-C1 (*"A larger group containing companies that are not genuine comparables is worse than a smaller correct one"*) and SM-C3 (*"'Unclassified' is a correct answer"*) both name a plausible optimisation and forbid it. The MVP scope kind is coherently *problem-solving* — one question, one input, one answer — and §4.1's *"The entire setup is an organisation number"* holds throughout.

### Findings
- **medium** The Vision's headline promise has no metric (§1 / §9) — *"any company can see itself in context in under a minute"* is the product's own claim, and SM-5 measures assembly latency while SM-8 measures that nothing but an organisation number is needed. Neither measures time to first insight. FR-6's note spots this but leaves it unresolved. *Fix:* add an SM for time from organisation number entered to first kroner amount displayed, or delete the claim from §1.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

Where the PRD is careful it is very careful. FR-22 is a model of the form: the floor is quantified (10), the counting rule is stated (*"per key figure, after companies with an undefined value for that figure are excluded"*), the user-visible behaviour is specified (row persists, aggregate cells state the count against ten), the downstream consequence is drawn (no kroner amount), and the test is named (*"Synthetic cohorts at and below 10 assert that no aggregate escapes"*). FR-30, FR-32, FR-53 and FR-67 are at the same standard. FR-30's consequence is unusually good engineering input: *"no total presented to the user, or contained in an export, sums more than one of {operating margin, cost of goods share, personnel cost share, other operating cost share}."*

But the quality is unevenly distributed, and it thins out precisely on the feature §4.2 calls *"the feature the product lives or dies on."* Stage 3 (FR-9) and the two adjustment affordances (FR-17, FR-18) are one sentence each with no consequences. Nowhere in the PRD is a **size band** defined — FR-7 filters on *"industry code and size band"*, FR-9 segments *"by size"*, and the reader is never told what a band is, how many there are, or what the boundaries are. FR-9's *"geography where the industry calls for it"* is unimplementable as written: which industries, which geography, at what granularity. §4.7 is thinner still, and it is load-bearing — FR-50 is a single sentence, yet FR-64 (*"A saved analysis shows the read date it was computed from"*) and FR-66 (*"A saved analysis that included it keeps its own figures"*) both depend on saved-analysis snapshot semantics FR-50 never states.

One outright contradiction and one unmodelled interaction are the material risks here.

### Findings
- **critical** FR-24 contradicts FR-22 (§4.3 FR-24 vs FR-22 / §11.8) — FR-24: *"A key figure is shown only if it can be computed for the subject and for at least the minimum group size of peers from the same source."* FR-22: below the floor *"the row persists and still shows the subject's own value."* OQ-8 was closed in favour of FR-22, but FR-24 was not updated. An implementer following FR-24 hides the row; one following FR-22 keeps it. FR-24's actual intent — visible only in its single consequence, *"A figure the subject has from OCR and the peers do not is not shown"* — is about source symmetry, not group size. *Fix:* restate FR-24 as a source-symmetry rule only ("a key figure is computed for the subject and its peers from the same source; where the subject's value is OCR-sourced and the peers' is not, no comparison is offered") and delete the group-size clause, which FR-22 already owns.
- **high** FR-25 and FR-22 collapse the peer group during the Norwegian filing window (§4.3 FR-25, FR-22 / §8) — FR-25 fixes the benchmark year as *"the subject's latest filed year"* and excludes *"a peer without a filing for that year."* §8 notes that *"Norwegian annual accounts cluster in a single filing window."* An early-filing subject therefore gets a group that cannot clear the 10-peer floor on any figure, and every row degrades at once — the single most likely way a first-visit analysis looks broken. Nothing in §4.3, §5 or §11 addresses it. *Fix:* FR-25 gains a consequence stating what happens when the subject's latest year has too few peer filings — fall back to the most recent year where the floor is met for the core figures, and say so on screen with both years named.
- **high** FR-9, FR-14, FR-17 and FR-18 have no testable consequence, on the feature the product lives or dies on (§4.2) — FR-9 ships with no consequences and two undefined terms; FR-14 says a disagreement *"is flagged rather than resolved"* without saying to whom, where, or how the flag relates to FR-16's three match-basis tiers (none of which is "disagreement"); FR-17 and FR-18 specify no visible result, no limit on how far a group may be reduced, and no interaction with FR-22's floor. *Fix:* give FR-9 explicit size-band boundaries and name the industries where geography applies; give FR-14 a user-visible surface and say whether a flagged candidate is included, excluded or listed separately; give FR-17/FR-18 consequences covering the floor interaction and whether an adjusted group persists across sessions.
- **high** FR-50 is load-bearing for FR-64 and FR-66 but specifies nothing (§4.7 FR-50) — *"A saved analysis accumulates history, so a later visit shows movement against the peer group rather than a fresh start."* What is snapshotted (figures only? the peer group membership? the user's exclusions? the closable share? the read date?), at what granularity, and what happens when the peer group changes underneath is undefined — yet FR-64 and FR-66 both make promises about a saved analysis's contents. *Fix:* FR-50 gains consequences enumerating exactly what a snapshot contains, and states that peer group membership is frozen at save time (which is what FR-66 already assumes).
- **medium** Twelve of sixty-seven FRs carry no Consequences block (§4) — FR-9, 14, 17, 18, 35, 36, 39, 46, 49, 50, 54, 56. Some are self-evident (FR-49, FR-56); several are not. FR-46's *"revokes access immediately"* has no bound and no test despite being an authorisation claim. FR-36's *"user-entered figures are kept only as history"* has no stated behaviour on conflict. FR-54 (*"does not prescribe action"*) is the hardest FR in the document to test and carries no test at all, while its sibling FR-53 carries a named automated one. *Fix:* add at least one consequence to each; for FR-54, either name the check (a review gate, a prohibited-phrase assertion) or mark it explicitly as a copy guideline rather than a testable requirement.
- **medium** No minimum subject size, so micro-company behaviour is undefined (§4.1 FR-3, §6.1) — §6.1's coverage arithmetic rests on companies *"with five or more employees"*, matching the screening population, but FR-3's gate tests only `naeringskode1.kode`. 62.100 has 9959 active AS against 1003 with five or more employees, so the large majority of possible subjects fall outside the peer population, and neither §2.2 Non-Users nor §5 excludes them. *Fix:* FR-3 states the subject-size condition, and either §2.2 adds micro-companies as non-users or FR-4's uncovered-industry message is extended to cover "no comparable group at this size."
- **medium** *"within a few seconds"* is an adjective where every neighbouring bound is numeric (§7, §9 SM-5) — SM-5 promises to validate it, which is not possible as stated. *Fix:* state a number, as FR-20 does for assembly.

## Scope honesty — strong

§5 is the best section in the document for this dimension. Omissions carry reasons, and the reasons are the real ones: *"A composite performance score … would require arbitrary weights across ratios and could not be traced to the accounts"*; screening excluded because *"on an open route, the most direct way to harvest the register"*; share links excluded because *"A link is a bearer token: it gets forwarded, pasted into chat and leaked through logs."* The **"Known limitations accepted rather than fixed"** block is rarer still — VAT overstating receivable days by up to 25 %, cost-line misclassification between the three shares, employee counts rejected for the right period — each *"disclosed rather than silently corrected."* §5's **Excluded key figures** paragraph even gives reasons per exclusion rather than a list.

De-scoping is done out loud. §6.2 names the third industry as *"intended, not committed"*, and §6.2's **Numeric quality targets** entry refuses to invent a threshold: *"v1 commits to measuring and reporting these, not to hitting a threshold."* §2.2 says *"Explicitly deferred, not served badly"* and means it.

Open-items density is proportionate for a green-light-to-build coursework PRD: four genuinely open questions, all gated on scheduled work, plus three pre-deployment gates that are correctly labelled as not v1 requirements. The two live assumptions in §12 are both narrow and both about presentation, not substance.

### Findings
- **medium** §0 promises inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags; there are none (§0 / §12) — *"Assumptions are tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` inline and indexed in §12"* is false: the only occurrences of the string are that sentence and §12's own preamble. §12's six entries reference sections (§9, §2.3, FR-28, FR-38, §6.2) that carry no tag, so a reader working through §4 or §9 has no signal that an assumption is in play at the point it bites. *Fix:* either place the tags inline at the locations §12 names, or rewrite §0 to describe §12 as a standalone register.
- **low** No `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` inline callouts (§4) — the rubric's marker is absent, but §5, §6.2, the per-UJ *"Deliberately not this"* / *"Scope boundary"* / *"What it deliberately will not do"* blocks, and the negative consequences inside FRs (FR-4, FR-32, FR-37) cover the same ground more thoroughly than tags would. Noted, not a defect.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This is a chain-top PRD feeding UX, architecture, epics and stories, so traceability carries real weight. The mechanics are clean: **FR-1 through FR-67 with no gaps and no duplicates**, deliberate out-of-order placement governed by a stated policy (*"An FR number is stable and never reused"*), and every one of the ~70 inline FR cross-references resolves to an existing FR. UJ and SM sets are contiguous (UJ-1–4, SM-1–8, SM-C1–4), every SM names what it validates, and every FR that realises a journey says which. The Glossary is genuinely load-bearing rather than decorative — *"Adviser — **not a role.** A user with many workspaces"* pre-empts the mistake FR-43 then tests for, and the register-field paragraph carries both API misspellings with *"do not 'fix' either"* attached.

`addendum.md` is doing the right job: it points into the four authoritative documents rather than restating them, and its **For UX** list hands `bmad-ux` six concrete unsolved design problems (*"The empty row"*, subordinating cost shares so *"nobody reaches for a calculator and adds them anyway"*) that are exactly the gaps the PRD leaves by design.

What blocks clean source-extraction is a layer that exists in the metrics but in no requirement.

### Findings
- **high** SM-1 measures an embeddings layer that no FR defines (§9 SM-1, §10 / §4.2) — SM-1's ablation is *"industry code and size alone, then adding the fingerprint, then embeddings, then model classification"* and claims to validate *"FR-7 to FR-16"* — but the funnel in §4.2 has five stages, the Glossary's funnel entries list five, and neither mentions embeddings. §10 goes further (*"If embeddings do not measurably beat model classification, classification stays"*), implying a built component. It is in `technical-note-architecture.md` (pgvector, line 81), so this is the PRD under-reaching its own authoritative source, not an invention. A story writer sourcing from FRs would not build it, and SM-1 would then be unmeasurable as specified. *Fix:* add an FR for the embeddings layer in §4.2 with its eligibility effect stated, or drop embeddings from SM-1 and §10 and cite the technical note for the fuller ablation.
- **medium** No FR states the interface is Norwegian (§4, §6.1, §11.2) — it appears as an §6.1 scope bullet, an answered open question and a §12 resolved assumption, but §4.9 covers only reflow and container scrolling. `AGENTS.md` makes it a hard rule (*"All user-facing copy is written in Norwegian"*), and copy is generated by the model in FR-52, so nothing binds generated text to bokmål. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-52 and to §4.9 requiring Norwegian user-facing copy, including generated explanation.
- **low** FR-5 cites the latency FR for an ingestion claim (§4.1 FR-5) — *"Every other classification happens at ingestion (FR-20)."* FR-20 is "Peer group assembly latency"; it happens to assert that no peer is classified at request time, but the requirement that classification occurs at ingestion is FR-11's territory. *Fix:* cite FR-11 and FR-59.
- **low** §4.3's *"Around a dozen measures"* is vague where the Glossary is exact (§4.3 description) — the Glossary and `docs/key-figures.md` both fix the count at 14 numbered plus cash share. *Fix:* say "the 14 numbered key figures plus cash share."

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product. Peerless has three distinct stakeholders in one workspace (owner, viewer, and an adviser who is deliberately not a role), so named-protagonist UJs are load-bearing rather than ceremonial — and they are, since UJ-3 is the only place the same-owner isolation requirement in FR-45 comes from. Four UJs is the right density: not one per feature, and each ends somewhere the product is genuinely limited.

The coursework dimension is handled without distorting the product PRD. §10 exists *"because Peerless is coursework as well as a product"*, and rather than bolting on academic language it states the claim, the baseline, the ablation and the contamination risk — which is also what a product team would want. Rigor is calibrated up, not down, and correctly: money handling, authorisation and the no-calculation rule each get a named test rather than a principle.

Folding NFRs into §4 as FRs (FR-20 latency, FR-55/56 responsiveness, FR-64–66 disclosure and licence) alongside the cross-cutting §7 could read as duplication, but here it helps: those items need to appear in epics, and §7 keeps the cross-cutting statement. The brownfield/greenfield question does not arise — there is no code yet, and the PRD correctly makes no claims about existing behaviour.

No findings.
